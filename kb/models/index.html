    Parameters of each model¶

    The base model has 10 free parameters (7 stellar, 2 remnant, the distance) and 3 profiled count nuisances. The dark-matter variants add a halo; the Jeans model has its own 10 parameters.

    Model Parameters beyond the base model Range Fixed
    Free cored halo \(\rho_{20}\) (DM density at 20 pc), \(r_s\) (scale) 0–10 M☉ pc⁻³; 5–150 pc, log \(\gamma=0\) (core), taper \(r_t=1\) kpc
    \(\rho_{20}\) profile \(r_s\) 5–150 pc, log \(\rho_{20}\) = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4
    Mass-per-star scan \(\rho_{20}\), \(r_s\) as the free halo HST count amplitude pinned: 8–12 M☉ per counted star

    Halo density: \(\rho(r)=\rho_{20}\,(r/20\,{\rm pc})^{-\gamma}\,[(1+r/r_s)/(1+20/r_s)]^{\gamma-3}\,e^{-(r^2-20^2)/r_t^2}\).

    What the data constrain. Twelve mocks drawn from the base model recover the total mass to ±3% at 20 pc and ±5% over 2–40 pc, but the recovered \(\rho_{20}\) scatters by 0.5–0.9 M☉ pc⁻³. The leading degeneracy is stellar mass against dark mass (remnants plus halo) through the count amplitude. Pinning the mass per counted star removes it but needs that number to ~3%; the photometric mass function gives 8.3–9.8 M☉ per star against the dynamical 10.7.

    Implementation and history.

    • Regularized DF implementation: the DF family, numerics and tests.
    • Anisotropy diagnostics: why the model needs two anisotropy transitions (inner data want radial, outer data tangential anisotropy).
    • Observed-data flexibility: the one-transition DF fails (χ²/N ≈ 4.8).
    • Compact DF proposal (PDF), compact DF recovery, frequency-mapper diagnostic.
    • Earlier mixture DF (superseded): AGAMA DF models, capacity challenge, mass recovery.
    • DF model literature review.
    • Next steps under discussion: Codex review, 2026-10-13 (multi-mass stellar populations, remnant treatment).

    Earlier: spherical Jeans ladder (historical)¶

    The first modelling sequence used the project's spherical anisotropic Jeans solver, with JamPy as a cross-check, and nested sampling. It is superseded by the DF models, which guarantee a positive distribution function.
