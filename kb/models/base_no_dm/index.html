    Base model: two-transition DF, no dark matter¶

    The reference model for the cluster mass distribution. Every dark-matter variant is compared with it.

    Status (2026-10-13)

    Refitted on 2026-10-13 with the corrected (area-weighted) count likelihood: results/df/dftwo_counts_area_20261013, best job observed_no_halo_start3. The fit is numerically validated but fails its own data-fit gate. It supersedes the 2026-09-23 fit (results/df/dftwo_counts_20260923), whose star-weighted count averages were biased; parameters moved by ≤ 4% and the mass per counted star by −1.8%.

    All parameters¶

    The fit is a multi-start optimisation (no posterior sampling), so best-fit values are given without uncertainties; mocks drawn from this model recover \(M_\star\) to ±6% and \(M_{\rm rem}\) to ±23% (see below).

    Stellar DF¶

    The regularized exponential action DF (contour construction of Binney 2026, MNRAS 549, stag854), rebuilt at every iteration and iterated to self-consistency in the total potential (stars + remnants):

    \[ f(\mathbf J)=A\,\exp\!\left[-\left(L_c/J_0\right)^{\alpha}\right], \qquad q=J_r+L . \]

    The contour label \(L_c\) follows the spherical frequency ratio, biased by the anisotropy function \(b(q)\): \(b>0\) favours radial orbits, \(b<0\) tangential ones. Two transitions:

    \[ b(q)=b_{\rm out}\,s(q;J_a)+\left(b_{\rm outer}-b_{\rm out}\right)s(q;J_{\rm outer}), \qquad s(q;J)=\frac{q^2}{q^2+J^2}. \]

    The core is isotropic (\(b\to0\) as \(q\to0\)); the model is radially anisotropic at intermediate radii and tangential outside.

    How the stellar mass is determined¶

    • Seven stellar parameters: the mass normalisation \(M_\star\) and the six DF parameters (\(J_0\), \(\alpha\), \(b_{\rm out}\), \(J_a\), \(b_{\rm outer}\), \(J_{\rm outer}\)). The stellar density is not an input: it is computed from \(f(\mathbf J)\) in the total potential, so the envelope and the anisotropy parameters together set its shape.
    • The star counts constrain the shape only. Their amplitude (counted stars per unit model surface density) is a nuisance parameter, profiled analytically at every evaluation; the counts say where the stars are, not how much mass they carry.
    • \(M_\star\) is set by the kinematics, jointly with the remnants. It is not taken from photometry. The mass per counted star (10.48 M☉ per F625W < 19 star) is therefore an output of the fit.
    • The only place \(M_\star\) is fixed externally is the mass-per-star scan, where the count amplitude is held at a value from the photometric mass function instead of being profiled.
    • Mass-to-light ratio. With L_V = 1.16×10⁶ L☉ (Harris catalogue V = 3.68 and E(B−V) = 0.12, at the fitted 5.356 kpc), \(M_\star/L_V = 2.6\) and \(M_{\rm tot}/L_V = 2.8\).

    Remnant component¶

    \[ \rho_{\rm rem}(r)=\frac{3M_{\rm rem}}{4\pi a_{\rm rem}^3} \left(1+\frac{r^2}{a_{\rm rem}^2}\right)^{-5/2} \]

    It stands for all mass that does not follow the counted light: neutron stars and stellar-mass black holes, any mass-segregated white dwarfs and heavy stars, and part of the effect of a central black hole. At the best fit it holds 8% of the total mass, has a half-mass radius of \(1.305\,a_{\rm rem}=2.8\) pc, and dominates the mass inside ~1.5 pc (60% inside 1 pc).

    Comparison with the literature: published fits find 1.35–2.65×10⁵ M☉ in a central non-luminous component; Bañares-Hernández+2025 fit the same Plummer form and get 2.65×10⁵ M☉ with a 1.88 pc scale. Details: Remnant mass and spatial scale.

    Is this remnant mass physically sensible? It is 8.4% of the total and 0.09 of the stellar mass, inside the IMF budget for remnants heavier than the tracer stars (8.7–11.8% with all black holes retained): Remnant mass and the stellar population.

    Distance, geometry, rotation¶

    • Distance free in 4.8–6.0 kpc with a Gaussian prior \(N(5.43, 0.05)\) kpc; best fit 5.356 kpc (1.5σ low).
    • Spherical and non-rotating. The published rotation is removed at the moment level, \(\sigma^2_{\rm pred}=\langle v^2\rangle_{\rm DF}-v^2_{\rm rot}\); a negative result is rejected, never floored.

    Comparison with the Jeans model (dashed curves)¶

    Full description, figures and posterior: Jeans comparison model.

    The dashed curves are the best rung-2 spherical Jeans fit without dark matter (results/fits/rung2_K1_turnover), the best model of the earlier Jeans ladder. Both models are fitted to the same 89-bin kinematic snapshot, so their kinematic χ² compare directly; the Jeans fit uses a fixed light profile (an MGE of the Trager+1995 profile joined to HST star counts) instead of the Poisson star counts.

    • The DF fits better overall (Δχ² = 34) and in every dataset except the HST tangential proper motions.
    • The Jeans model follows the central rise of the HST proper-motion dispersions inside 0.3 pc (~11″), which the DF, with an isotropic core and no black hole, stays below by 1.5–2.4σ in several bins: χ² = 7.3 (Jeans) against 21.5 (DF) for those 10 bins. This is where the black hole matters.
    • It pays for this at intermediate radii: at 2–20 pc (36 bins of HST, MUSE and Gaia) χ² = 122.2 (Jeans) against 77.8 (DF), mostly in the HST radial proper motions (40.2 against 20.2) and the MUSE line-of-sight dispersions (30.9 against 18.7).
    • The black hole is tied to an imposed anisotropy. A cored stellar density around a point mass needs β₀ ≤ −½ for any non-negative DF; the prior enforces it and the fit sits at the bound (β₀ = −0.503). Even so, under the standard separable construction a non-negative DF fails to exist at 0.02–0.18 pc, where the black hole dominates (89.7% of posterior draws fail; no failure outside ~0.2 pc; a non-separable DF is not excluded; DF consistency audit). The DF base model is non-negative by construction.
    • Both put about the same total mass in the cluster (3.2–3.3×10⁶ M☉) but divide it differently: the Jeans fit has 20% less stellar mass and nearly three times more remnant mass, spread over a 2.5× larger scale. This is the same stars-versus-dark-mass degeneracy seen within the DF fits.
    • Why \(M_\star\) differs by 11% at the same total mass. In neither model is the stellar mass fixed by the light or the counts; it is the normalisation the kinematics leave after the remnants (and, for Jeans, the black hole and the imposed central anisotropy) take their share. The remnant mass is close to the total minus \(M_\star\), so with remnants at 8–17% of the total, a 1% change in \(M_\star\) moves \(M_{\rm rem}\) by ~10%.
    • A central black hole was not supported by the DF fits on binned data (September step 3); whether the inner rise needs one, or radially biased central orbits, or mass segregation, is not settled.

    What has been tested¶

    • Convergence: 4 of 6 starts valid (2 Latin starts infeasible), all four reach the same minimum (objectives within 0.01); no parameter at a bound.
    • Numerics: grid refinement shifts predictions by 0.0005σ; direct projection checks agree to ≤ 5×10⁻⁴ in Σ, LOS, PM_R and PM_T.
    • Estimator recovery (2026-09-23 fit, old count likelihood): 12 mocks drawn from this model with published kinematic noise and Poisson counts recover the total mass to ±3% at 20 pc and ±5% over 2–40 pc, \(M_\star\) to ±6%, \(M_{\rm rem}\) to ±23%.

    Not yet tested: mocks whose truth breaks the model's own assumptions (mass segregation, rotation, another remnant shape); calibration of the error bars.

    Built-in assumptions¶

    1. Mass follows light: one stellar DF is both tracer and mass.
    2. Isotropic core.
    3. One remnant component with a fixed Plummer shape.
    4. No central black hole.
    5. Spherical; rotation subtracted from published curves.
    6. Published error bars taken as they are.

    Reproduce¶

    source <venv>/bin/activate
export PYTHONPATH=src
python bin/run_compact_df_recovery.py prepare-real --out results/df/<new_label> \
  --two-transition --branches no_halo \
  --counts ocen_counts_hst_f625w19,ocen_counts_gaia_g17 \
  --free-distance 4.8:6.0 --distance-prior 5.43:0.05 \
  --bound stellar.J0=30:800 --bound stellar.J_a=5:500 --bound stellar.b_out=-2:2 \
  --hand-starts 2 --n-starts 4 --probe-workers 5
python bin/run_compact_df_recovery.py run --out results/df/<new_label> --workers 2 --max-calls 600

    Each run uses 2 jobs × (5 probe workers + 1) = 12 cores and took ~30 min in September. The batch freezes its source, configuration and inputs; a run directory is never reused.

    Code: src/ocen_dm/kinematics/regularized_df.py (DF, remnants, halo), df_fit.py (joint problem), counts.py (count likelihood), compact_recovery.py (mocks, residuals), bin/run_compact_df_recovery.py (driver).
