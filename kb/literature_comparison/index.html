    Comparison with published mass models¶

    How our two no-DM models (the DF base model and the Jeans comparison model) compare with published models of Omega Centauri's mass distribution. Published numbers are taken from the papers' abstracts (checked in ADS, 2026-10-14) unless marked otherwise; all papers are in the SciX library.

    Summary

    • Total mass and M/L agree within the spread of published values. Our total M/L_V of 2.8 is close to Watkins+2013 (2.71); our total mass, 3.27×10⁶ M☉ (DF best fit, M★ + M_rem), is 13–22% below the recent estimates of 3.7–4.2×10⁶ M☉ (D'Souza & Rix 2013 scaled to our distance; the Baumgardt database).
    • The central dark mass is where the literature splits, and our two models fall on the two sides. The Jeans model's black hole (posterior median 4.25×10⁴ M☉; 3.67×10⁴ M☉ with the star-count tracer, J-counts) reproduces the classic IMBH claims (Noyola+2008/2010, Baumgardt 2017, Jalali+2012). The DF model's compact remnant component (2.7×10⁵ M☉, 2.2 pc scale, 8% of the mass, no black hole) matches the "extended central mass" picture: 2–3×10⁵ M☉ favoured by kinematics plus pulsar timing (Bañares-Hernández+2025: 2.65×10⁵ M☉ in a Plummer of 1.88 pc, the same form, mass and nearly the same scale as ours) and ~5% of the mass in stellar-mass black holes (Zocchi+2019, Baumgardt+2019, Dickson+2024). The remnant mass is weakly constrained: the tied, completeness-corrected DF refit gives 2.00×10⁵ M☉ (1.88 pc) with predictions that differ by a few per cent, and J-counts puts 8.4×10⁵ M☉ in remnants (posterior median).
    • Anisotropy: the DF profile (isotropic core, mildly radial at intermediate radii, tangential outside) agrees with oMEGACat VI and van de Ven+2006; the Jeans model's tangential core does not.
    • The fast-moving stars require a point mass of at least 8,200 M☉ (Häberle+2024, robust bound). Their model-dependent estimates go higher (≥ 21,100 M☉ with accelerations; 39,000–47,000 M☉ preferred by an N-body comparison, which brackets our Jeans black hole). With a fixed point mass the DF still fits (DF + central black hole): 8,200 M☉ lowers the objective by 5.5; 4×10⁴ M☉ fits the central kinematics better (χ²_kin −12) but over-predicts the central HST counts (+15), so the objective falls by only 1.0. Neither difference is calibrated.

    D'Souza & Rix give \(4.55\times10^6\,(D/5.5\,{\rm kpc})^3\) M☉ (spherical) and \(4.05\times10^6\,(D/5.5\,{\rm kpc})^3\) M☉ (flattened); at 5.356 kpc the factor is 0.923.

    • M/L is close to published values: our total M/L_V of 2.8 is slightly above Watkins (2.71) and van de Ven (2.5). Scaled to our distance with M/L ∝ D their values would be 3.2 and 2.8; their distances were fitted jointly, so this scaling is only indicative. Our total masses are M★ + M_rem (+ M_BH for Jeans).

    • Our total mass is 13–22% below the recent estimates at our distance (3.27 against 3.74–4.20×10⁶ M☉ from D'Souza & Rix and 3.94×10⁶ M☉ from the Baumgardt database). van de Ven+2006 found 2.5×10⁶ M☉ at 4.8 kpc. Possible reasons, not yet tested: our model is spherical (D'Souza & Rix find flattening lowers the mass by 10%, in the same direction, so it does not explain it); our kinematics reach ~60 pc and the mass beyond is extrapolated; the Gaia data may have systematics in the outer bins; and the Baumgardt value comes from mass-segregated N-body models.

    • Distance: ours (5.356 kpc) is pulled by the prior; the oMEGACat VI kinematic distance is 5.494 ± 0.061 kpc (Häberle+2025), 2.2σ larger. At 5.494 kpc our proper-motion masses would be ~8% higher.

    Häberle+2024 seven fast-moving stars in the central 3″ (0.08 pc), multi-epoch HST; projected velocities 66–113 km/s against a no-IMBH escape velocity of 62 km/s IMBH ≥ 8,200 M☉ (robust bound: velocities and escape velocity only). Model-dependent: ≥ 21,100 M☉ (99%) with the measured accelerations (all consistent with zero within 3σ); an N-body comparison of the inner 10″ prefers 39,000–47,000 M☉ and disfavours no IMBH, a stellar-mass black-hole cluster and > 50,000 M☉, with the authors' caveat of low number statistics (full text, main text p. 3)
    Bañares-Hernández+2025 Jeans (GravSphere) + millisecond-pulsar accelerations extended central mass ~2–3×10⁵ M☉ favoured over an IMBH; IMBH < 6×10³ M☉ (3σ)
    • Our two models reproduce the two sides of the debate with the same data. The black-hole mass of our Jeans model is the classic value (4–5×10⁴ M☉). That value comes from models with isotropic or imposed central anisotropy; in ours the black hole is tied to β₀ at its −½ bound and gains Δχ² ≈ 6 from a few bins inside 0.3 pc.
    • The DF model's dark mass is the "extended central mass". Its remnant component, 2.7×10⁵ M☉ with a 2.2 pc scale (half-mass radius 2.8 pc), matches the 2–3×10⁵ M☉ that Bañares-Hernández+2025 favour from kinematics and pulsar timing, the ~5% stellar-mass black-hole fraction of Zocchi+2019, Baumgardt+2019 and Dickson+2024, and the ≲ 5×10⁵ M☉ remnant core that Evans+2022 allow. Our component is a generic Plummer, so it can also contain white dwarfs and neutron stars.
    • The fast-moving stars set a robust lower bound on a point mass. Häberle+2024 find ≥ 8,200 M☉ from the velocities and escape velocity alone. This already exceeds the 3σ upper limit of Bañares-Hernández+2025 (6×10³ M☉): the published results are in tension. Their model-dependent estimates are higher: ≥ 21,100 M☉ (99%) when the accelerations (all within 3σ of zero) are modelled, and 39,000–47,000 M☉ preferred by a comparison with N-body models of the inner 10″, which also disfavours a stellar-mass black-hole cluster without an IMBH; the authors caution that low number statistics limit that comparison. The 21,100 M☉ limit would also exceed the 3σ limit of van der Marel & Anderson 2010 (1.8×10⁴ M☉).
    • Our two models map onto that tension. The Jeans black hole (posterior median 4.25×10⁴ M☉; 3.67×10⁴ M☉ for J-counts) lies inside or near Häberle's preferred range; the DF model's extended remnants match Bañares-Hernández. The DF fitted with a fixed point mass (DF + central black hole, 2026-10-15) gives objective 275.2 at 8,200 M☉ and 279.7 at 4×10⁴ M☉, against 280.7 without; the 4×10⁴ M☉ cusp over-predicts the central HST counts. These fits use the 2026-10-13 count likelihood and are not calibrated with mocks.

    • The DF remnant component reproduces the Bañares-Hernández central mass: the same mass within 3%, a scale 12% larger once both are at the same distance, and the same mass fraction. They reach it with a different method and with pulsar timing; from stellar kinematics alone they find a smaller and more compact component (2.20×10⁵ M☉, 1.59 pc).
    • The black-hole-only estimates are lower, 1.35–1.82×10⁵ M☉ (4.5–5.9%). That is expected: our component also holds whatever neutron stars and segregated white dwarfs do not follow the light, whereas those models put white dwarfs and neutron stars in the visible or light-traced mass (in Dickson+2024 all dark remnants make up 50% of the mass).
    • Scales: besides Bañares-Hernández, only Evans+2022 constrain one (r_max ≲ 1 pc for an NFW), and Zocchi+2019 give a qualitative extent (to about the half-mass radius, 8.6 pc). The concentration differs between models: within 0.2 pc Zocchi's black-hole component holds ~1.4×10³ M☉, against ~300 M☉ for the Bañares-Hernández Plummer and ~200 M☉ for ours (computed for the Plummer models).
    • The Jeans model's remnant component (5.4×10⁵ M☉ at 5.3 pc) is twice as massive and 2.5 times more extended than any of these; its central mass is carried by the black hole instead.
    • The agreement depends on the fit. The tied, completeness-corrected refit gives 2.00×10⁵ M☉ in a 1.88 pc Plummer: 25% below the Bañares-Hernández mass, with a scale 3% below theirs at our distance (1.94 pc), while its predictions differ from the base fit by a few per cent.
    • Our remnant mass is the least constrained of our numbers. It trades against the stellar mass (±23% on mocks), pinning the mass per counted star between 8 and 12 M☉ moves it from 1.6 to 4.4×10⁵ M☉, the tied refit moves it by −27%, and J-counts puts 8.4×10⁵ M☉ in remnants.

    Anisotropy¶

    Study Anisotropy
    This work, DF base model isotropic core; radial, peak β ≈ +0.15 near 8 pc; tangential beyond ~22 pc (−0.53 at 100 pc)
    This work, Jeans model tangential core (β₀ ≈ −0.52, imposed ≤ −½); radial +0.15 at 3–15 pc; tangential outside
    Häberle+2025 (oMEGACat VI) isotropic in the core, increasing radial anisotropy outwards (HST field)
    Watkins+2013 mildly radial, β = 0.10 ± 0.02 (single value)
    van de Ven+2006 close to isotropic inside ~10′, increasingly tangential outside
    Zocchi+2017 radially anisotropic models fit the central kinematics
    • The DF profile matches the published picture: isotropic core, radially biased within the HST field, tangential in the outskirts. Our zero crossing (~22 pc, 14′ at our distance) is near van de Ven's ~10′ transition.
    • The Jeans model's tangential core disagrees with the isotropic core measured directly by oMEGACat VI. It is a consequence of the black hole (a cored tracer around a point mass needs β₀ ≤ −½), which is a further argument against taking its black-hole mass at face value.

    Dark matter¶

    Evans+2022 find a non-luminous component of ≲ 10⁶ M☉ within the half-light radius strongly preferred over a constant-M/L stellar model and leave open that part of it is dark matter. Our models agree that a centrally concentrated non-luminous mass is needed (2.7×10⁵ M☉ in the DF model, ~6×10⁵ M☉ of remnants plus black hole in the Jeans model), but find that it can be supplied by remnants without dark matter. Our dark-matter variants show that the data constrain the total mass profile, not how it splits between stars, remnants and dark matter (Results).

    Gaps¶

    • No comparison yet with published axisymmetric or rotating models on our data; ours are spherical.
    • The published total masses use different data and extents; a like-for-like test (our pipeline on their data, or theirs on ours) has not been done.
    • Earlier literature numbers assembled for the Jeans fits: Literature baselines.
