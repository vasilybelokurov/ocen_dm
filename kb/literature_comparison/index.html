    Comparison with published mass models¶

    How our two no-DM models (the DF base model and the Jeans comparison model) compare with published models of Omega Centauri's mass distribution. Published numbers are taken from the papers' abstracts (checked in ADS, 2026-10-14) unless marked otherwise; all papers are in the SciX library.

    Summary

    • Total mass and M/L agree within the spread of published values. Our total M/L_V of 2.8 is close to Watkins+2013 (2.71); our total mass, 3.26×10⁶ M☉, is 13–22% below the recent estimates of 3.7–4.2×10⁶ M☉ (D'Souza & Rix 2013 scaled to our distance; the Baumgardt database).
    • The central dark mass is where the literature splits, and our two models fall on the two sides. The Jeans model's 4.3×10⁴ M☉ black hole reproduces the classic IMBH claims (Noyola+2008/2010, Baumgardt 2017, Jalali+2012). The DF model's compact remnant component (2.7×10⁵ M☉, 2.2 pc scale, 8% of the mass, no black hole) matches the "extended central mass" picture: 2–3×10⁵ M☉ favoured by kinematics plus pulsar timing (Bañares-Hernández+2025) and ~5% of the mass in stellar-mass black holes (Zocchi+2019, Baumgardt+2019, Dickson+2024).
    • Anisotropy: the DF profile (isotropic core, mildly radial at intermediate radii, tangential outside) agrees with oMEGACat VI and van de Ven+2006; the Jeans model's tangential core does not.
    • Not yet tested: whether the DF model accommodates the ≥ 8,200 M☉ black hole implied by the fast-moving stars (Häberle+2024).

    Total mass, mass-to-light ratio and distance¶

    Proper-motion-based masses scale as \(M\propto D^3\) and M/L as \(\propto D\); published values are given at their own distance, and scaled to ours (5.356 kpc) only where the paper states the scaling.

    Study Method D [kpc] Total mass [10⁶ M☉] M/L_V

    This work, Jeans model spherical Jeans, black hole, same kinematics 5.34–5.36 3.18–3.24 2.7–2.8 (stellar 2.0–2.3)
    van de Ven+2006 axisymmetric Schwarzschild, ground-based PMs + LOS 4.8 ± 0.3 (fitted) 2.5 ± 0.3 2.5 ± 0.1
    Watkins+2013 discrete axisymmetric Jeans 4.59 ± 0.08 (fitted) — 2.71 ± 0.05
    D'Souza & Rix 2013 discrete PM likelihood scaled to 5.356 4.20 (spherical isotropic), 3.74 (flattened) —
    Baumgardt GC database N-body grid (catalogue value; version unrecorded, UNVERIFIED against the live database) 5.43 3.94 —
    Merritt+1997 non-parametric, 469 LOS velocities — — mass "slightly more extended than" the light

    D'Souza & Rix give \(4.55\times10^6\,(D/5.5\,{\rm kpc})^3\) M☉ (spherical) and \(4.05\times10^6\,(D/5.5\,{\rm kpc})^3\) M☉ (flattened); at 5.356 kpc the factor is 0.923.

    • M/L is close to published values: our total M/L_V of 2.8 is slightly above Watkins (2.71) and van de Ven (2.5). Scaled to our distance with M/L ∝ D their values would be 3.2 and 2.8; their distances were fitted jointly, so this scaling is only indicative.
    • Our total mass is 13–22% below the recent estimates at our distance (3.26 against 3.74–4.20×10⁶ M☉ from D'Souza & Rix and 3.94×10⁶ M☉ from the Baumgardt database). van de Ven+2006 found 2.5×10⁶ M☉ at 4.8 kpc. Possible reasons, not yet tested: our model is spherical (D'Souza & Rix find flattening lowers the mass by 10%, in the same direction, so it does not explain it); our kinematics reach ~60 pc and the mass beyond is extrapolated; the Gaia data may have systematics in the outer bins; and the Baumgardt value comes from mass-segregated N-body models.
    • Distance: ours (5.356 kpc) is pulled by the prior; the oMEGACat VI kinematic distance is 5.494 ± 0.061 kpc (Häberle+2025), 2.2σ larger. At 5.494 kpc our proper-motion masses would be ~8% higher.

    The central dark mass¶

    Study Method Central dark mass
    This work, Jeans model spherical Jeans, β₀ ≤ −½ imposed IMBH 4.3 (3.6–4.9) ×10⁴ M☉ (95%)
    This work, DF base model self-consistent DF, isotropic core no IMBH; remnants 2.7×10⁵ M☉ in a 2.2 pc Plummer (8% of the mass)
    Noyola+2008 isotropic spherical, Gemini + HST IMBH 4.0 (+0.75/−1.0) ×10⁴ M☉
    Noyola+2010 VLT kinematics IMBH (4.7 ± 1.0) ×10⁴ M☉
    Jalali+2012 N-body, isotropic multimass King ICs IMBH 5×10⁴ M☉ (2% of the stellar mass)
    Baumgardt 2017 N-body grid IMBH ~4×10⁴ M☉
    van der Marel & Anderson 2010 anisotropic spherical Jeans, HST PMs core models fit with M_BH = 0; M_BH ≲ 1.2×10⁴ (1σ), ≲ 1.8×10⁴ M☉ (3σ)
    Zocchi+2017 radially anisotropic limepy central kinematics fitted without an IMBH
    Zocchi+2019 multimass limepy ~5% of the mass in stellar-mass black holes
    Baumgardt+2019 N-body grid + fast-star test no IMBH; 4.6% of the mass in a central cluster of stellar-mass black holes
    Evans+2022 axisymmetric Jeans, dark + luminous components non-luminous component ≲ 10⁶ M☉ within r_h preferred over constant M/L; a compact remnant core can supply up to ~5×10⁵ M☉
    Dickson+2024 multimass models black-hole mass fraction above 5%
    Häberle+2024 seven fast-moving stars in the central 3″ (0.08 pc) IMBH ≥ 8,200 M☉ (firm lower limit)
    Bañares-Hernández+2025 Jeans (GravSphere) + millisecond-pulsar accelerations extended central mass ~2–3×10⁵ M☉ favoured over an IMBH; IMBH < 6×10³ M☉ (3σ)
    • Our two models reproduce the two sides of the debate with the same data. The black-hole mass of our Jeans model is the classic value (4–5×10⁴ M☉). That value comes from models with isotropic or imposed central anisotropy; in ours the black hole is tied to β₀ at its −½ bound and gains Δχ² ≈ 6 from a few bins inside 0.3 pc.
    • The DF model's dark mass is the "extended central mass". Its remnant component, 2.7×10⁵ M☉ with a 2.2 pc scale (half-mass radius 2.8 pc), matches the 2–3×10⁵ M☉ that Bañares-Hernández+2025 favour from kinematics and pulsar timing, the ~5% stellar-mass black-hole fraction of Zocchi+2019, Baumgardt+2019 and Dickson+2024, and the ≲ 5×10⁵ M☉ remnant core that Evans+2022 allow. Our component is a generic Plummer, so it can also contain white dwarfs and neutron stars.
    • A small IMBH is not excluded by either picture. The fast-star lower limit (≥ 8,200 M☉, Häberle+2024) is 0.25% of the cluster mass. It lies below the 3σ upper limit of van der Marel & Anderson (1.8×10⁴ M☉) but above the 3σ upper limit of Bañares-Hernández+2025 (6×10³ M☉), a tension between those two published results. Our DF model has no point mass; adding one of ~10⁴ M☉ at fixed everything else is the natural next test.

    Anisotropy¶

    Study Anisotropy
    This work, DF base model isotropic core; radial, peak β ≈ +0.15 near 8 pc; tangential beyond ~22 pc (−0.53 at 100 pc)
    This work, Jeans model tangential core (β₀ ≈ −0.52, imposed ≤ −½); radial +0.15 at 3–15 pc; tangential outside
    Häberle+2025 (oMEGACat VI) isotropic in the core, increasing radial anisotropy outwards (HST field)
    Watkins+2013 mildly radial, β = 0.10 ± 0.02 (single value)
    van de Ven+2006 close to isotropic inside ~10′, increasingly tangential outside
    Zocchi+2017 radially anisotropic models fit the central kinematics
    • The DF profile matches the published picture: isotropic core, radially biased within the HST field, tangential in the outskirts. Our zero crossing (~22 pc, 14′ at our distance) is near van de Ven's ~10′ transition.
    • The Jeans model's tangential core disagrees with the isotropic core measured directly by oMEGACat VI. It is a consequence of the black hole (a cored tracer around a point mass needs β₀ ≤ −½), which is a further argument against taking its black-hole mass at face value.

    Dark matter¶

    Evans+2022 find a non-luminous component of ≲ 10⁶ M☉ within the half-light radius strongly preferred over a constant-M/L stellar model and leave open that part of it is dark matter. Our models agree that a centrally concentrated non-luminous mass is needed (2.7×10⁵ M☉ in the DF model, ~6×10⁵ M☉ of remnants plus black hole in the Jeans model), but find that it can be supplied by remnants without dark matter. Our dark-matter variants show that the data constrain the total mass profile, not how it splits between stars, remnants and dark matter (Results).

    Gaps¶

    • No comparison yet with published axisymmetric or rotating models on our data; ours are spherical.
    • The published total masses use different data and extents; a like-for-like test (our pipeline on their data, or theirs on ours) has not been done.
    • Earlier literature numbers assembled for the Jeans fits: Literature baselines.
