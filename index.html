    oCen_dm: dark matter in Omega Centauri¶

    Question. Can Omega Centauri's tidal debris distinguish an extended dark-matter halo from the stars, remnants and central mass that fit its internal kinematics?

    The project has three connected strands:

    1. Cluster mass model. Infer the present mass distribution from the internal kinematics and star counts with action-based distribution functions (DFs). The base no-DM model is the reference for every dark-matter variant.
    2. Dark-matter survival and tails. Estimate how much dark matter could survive the progenitor's disruption and what the tidal tails of no-DM and DM models look like (N-body).
    3. Stream 54 (Fimbulthul). Model the stream in a barred Milky Way with the LMC, as the external test of the cluster's mass and orbit.

    A joint kinematic-and-tail inference is the long-term goal and is not built.

    Status (2026-10-13)¶

    Strand Where it stands Read
    Cluster mass model A two-transition DF with Poisson star counts fits the 89 kinematic bins without dark matter (χ²_kin = 156), but fails its own data-fit gate. The data fix the total mass profile (±3% at 20 pc) but not the split between stars and dark mass; that split depends on the assumed stellar mass per counted star. A bias in the count likelihood was fixed on 2026-10-13 and the base model refitted (mass per counted star 10.67 → 10.48 M☉); the dark-matter scans have not been rerun. Base model, Models, write-up (PDF)
    DM survival and tails N-body tails of a no-DM and a DM model on Omega Cen's orbit agree to 10% in kinematics; the DM model loses 1.3–1.6× fewer stars. Neither difference is testable with published tail data. Results
    Stream 54 A rotating bar bends the stream part-way; the LMC shifts the distal proper motions by a few tenths of a mas/yr but is absorbed by the cluster's PM. The proper-motion "knee" stars do not connect to the cluster in the tested recent models. PM refinement, Results
