    The scaled-energy hypothesis is not supported as stated. AGAMA explicitly handles \(\Phi(0)=-\infty\): its coordinate [ X=\log[1/\Phi(0)-1/E] ] then becomes \(\log(-1/E)\). There is no automatic compression of weak binding energies caused by an infinite central potential. The grid is adaptive in log radius. See scaleE, actions_spherical.cpp:42, createInterpolationGrid, potential_utils.cpp:738, and the upstream implementation.

    Also, your frequency mode named "exact" still trusts finite, bound mapper outputs without checking their action residual. It inverts \(J_r(E,L)\) only for nonfinite or unbound outputs. Accurate quadrature at the wrong energy remains a possible error. regularized_df.py:119

    A. AGAMA has a direct route, but neither suggested option guarantees your required accuracy.

    • ActionFinder(potential, interp=False) does not disable spherical interpolation. The factory selects ActionFinderSpherical without consulting interpolate; that argument reaches the axisymmetric branch only. actions_factory.cpp:35
    • agama.actions(potential, points) selects direct spherical integration automatically. No method argument is needed. It dispatches to evalSpherical, which computes energy from the supplied potential, finds turning points, and integrates \(J_r\). interface_python.cpp:3724, actions_factory.cpp:20, actions_spherical.cpp:585
    • Direct does not mean arbitrarily accurate. Its quadrature order is capped at 40, with a heuristic documented for approximately constant accuracy down to \(r_p/r_a=10^{-3}\). That is inadequate assurance for your extreme eccentricities. actions_spherical.cpp:30, math_core.h:560

    I checked the installed binary in pure Kepler, \(M=8200\), \(J_r+L=100\):

    \(L/(J_r+L)\) agama.actions relative \(J_r\) error ActionFinder error
    0.001 \(+4.49\times10^{-4}\) \(+3.61\times10^{-4}\)
    0.01 \(-1.42\times10^{-5}\) \(-1.07\times10^{-3}\)
    0.3 \(+8.78\times10^{-13}\) \(-4.05\times10^{-7}\)

    interp=False reproduced the default finder. Thus the \(4.5\times10^{-4}\) Kepler error also exists without action interpolation.

    There are nevertheless concrete interpolation failure mechanisms worth investigating:

    • The finder computes energy using its internal interpolated potential, rather than the original composite. Your comparison therefore mixes potential-interpolation and action-interpolation errors. actions_spherical.cpp:649
    • It interpolates \(J_r/(L_c-L)\), clips coordinates to its table boundaries, and clamps negative results to zero. This provides a mechanism for spurious zero actions, although I have not established which mechanism caused your start-2 failure. actions_spherical.cpp:337, actions_spherical.cpp:73
    • The mapper extrapolates its energy table and applies one first-order correction. That is not a converged action inversion. actions_spherical.cpp:679, actions_spherical.cpp:725

    B. Establish an all-direct BH reference before adopting a hybrid.

    I would first use converged direct \(J_r\) quadrature throughout BH models. Use that implementation to determine where approximation is safe. Replacing only weakly bound actions prematurely assumes the error depends primarily on energy; the eccentricity dependence above already contradicts that simplification.

    Your existing helper needs hardening before becoming a general action backend: it assumes r0 lies inside the orbit, uses fixed-order quadrature, and clips negative radicands. Require valid brackets, explicit radial/circular limits, and convergence checks rather than treating every returned number as certified. regularized_df.py:179, regularized_df.py:213

    For a production hybrid:

    • Validate an error envelope over energy, circularity, and allowed potential parameters, including actual velocity quadrature nodes.
    • Choose the boundary from errors in density, pressures, projected predictions, and likelihood—not an arbitrary radius.
    • A hard switch is acceptable if the disagreement is negligible throughout the overlap. Otherwise moving model parameters can move quadrature nodes across a finite jump. Smooth blending removes that jump but does not remove bias or guarantee monotonic actions.

    Your velocity nodes are fixed in escape-speed fraction, so their physical energies change with the potential; a fixed Gauss rule does not eliminate this switching problem. positive_df.py:157

    If direct evaluation is too expensive, your own table is reasonable. However, AGAMA already uses Kepler-compatible scaled energy and circularity coordinates. The improvement must be validated table construction, refinement, and fallback, rather than merely choosing those coordinates again. actions_spherical.cpp:347

    C. Small action errors can matter, but their magnitude alone cannot establish fit bias.

    For your implemented exponential DF, at fixed amplitude, [ \delta\log f \simeq -\alpha(L_c/J_0)^\alpha\,\delta L_c/L_c. ] Errors can therefore be amplified in the steep tail. A relative error in \(J_r\), especially near \(J_r=0\), is not directly a relative error in dispersion. regularized_df.py:438

    The decisive test is:

    1. In the same frozen potential and DF, replace only the action calculation; compare density, both pressures, and actual bin predictions.
    2. Rebuild self-consistency with each backend and compare the full objective.
    3. Refit representative starts and the BH-mass profile; measure parameter shifts and likelihood changes.

    Use the joint prediction displacement [ D^2=\sum_i(\Delta\sigma_i/s_i)^2+ \sum_j(\Delta\mu_j)^2/\mu_j ] as an initial diagnostic, then evaluate the actual likelihood with its nuisance parameters treated consistently. The implemented objective combines kinematic residuals and count deviances; count normalizations and backgrounds may be profiled. df_fit.py:130, counts.py:155

    For scale: a coherent dispersion shift of \(0.1\%\) across 89 independent bins with \(2\%\) errors gives \(D^2\approx0.22\). That does not imply a \(0.1\%\) action error produces that shift.

    D. The main diagnostic traps are these.

    • Mapper frequency agreement can hide wrong actions. In Kepler the frequency ratio is independent of energy. In composites, verify the returned \(J_r(E,L)\) and actual angular momentum before trusting a bound mapper point. Your current fallback does not perform those checks. regularized_df.py:127
    • Export corruption extends beyond the BH. This AGAMA version writes analytic component types without their parameters. Plummer reconstruction defaults include mass=1, scaleRadius=1. Reloading can therefore change remnants as well as remove the point-mass singularity. Reconstruct every static component from configuration and verify potential/force equality before comparison. potential_factory.cpp:995, potential_factory.cpp:1032, potential_factory.cpp:158
    • rmax is not an orbit boundary. Multipole evaluates an asymptotic continuation outside its grid. Direct integration there can accurately measure the continuation rather than establish its physical accuracy. Test radial-domain convergence separately. potential_multipole.cpp:1478

    • Current validation shares the suspect backend. Closure uses self.af, and GalaxyModel receives that same finder; direct projected moments therefore do not independently validate actions. The BH action regression checks agama.actions only for \(c>0.1\) and \(q<10^5\), excluding the reported difficult regime. regularized_df.py:605, positive_df.py:338, test_regularized_df.py:318

    • Verified versus assumed. I read the cited project and local AGAMA sources, checked that the relevant local AGAMA source files match those shipped with the installed package, and ran read-only numerical comparisons. Your radial quadrature recovered the sampled Kepler actions accurately and exposed the direct-routine eccentricity errors above. I did not reproduce the base/start-2 failures, orbit integrations, or full fits. I assume your reported observations and uncertainties are accurate. The available AGAMA environment lacks SciPy, so I did not run the project suite.

    • Strongest argument against my recommendation. All-direct evaluation may impose substantial cost for errors that largely cancel in integrated observables. A validated hybrid could deliver indistinguishable inference much faster. The missing evidence is the full prediction-and-refit comparison, not further isolated action accuracy.

    • Checks needed for confidence. Confirm the actual fitting binary/version; reproduce start-2 from the live potential; separate internal-potential error from action-table error; establish quadrature convergence in composite potentials; test finite-but-wrong mapper outputs; verify export/reconstruction force equality; and compare rebuilt models, refits, and BH profiles against the direct reference.
